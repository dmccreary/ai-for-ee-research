## Gap Analysis

1. **Topic count and overlap (-1).** Topics 7 (planning and execution agents)
   and 8 (orchestration) cover closely related ground. They can stay separate
   for concept generation, but consider grouping the 16 topics under four or
   five themes for chapter planning.
2. **EE specificity in Apply (-1).** The outcomes read as generally applicable
   to any research field. Adding two or three EE and computer-design tasks
   (for example generating a testbench, scripting a parameter sweep, or
   cleaning measurement data) would anchor the learning graph in the
   audience's domain.
3. **Named case studies in Analyze (-1).** The case-study outcome refers to
   case studies generically. Naming them (for example the MicroSims arXiv
   paper and a specific intelligent textbook) would let the graph include
   concrete case-study concepts.

## Improvement Suggestions

Prioritized by their effect on concept generation:

1. Add two or three EE or computer-design examples to the Apply level.
2. Name the specific case studies once the talk content is final.
3. Optionally add a short "Course Format" section (duration, hands-on versus
   lecture, expected tools). The rubric does not score it, but it helps
   chapter design.
