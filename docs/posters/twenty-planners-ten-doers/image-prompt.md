## Critical Rule

**This image must contain absolutely no text of any kind.** No title, no headings, no labels, no letters, no numerals, no captions, no callout lines, no signs with writing, no logos, no watermark, and no readable code. Task cards, status boards, folders, screens, and papers show only unreadable abstract marks: blurred gray lines, plain color blocks, and simple pictograms. The attention meter has colored bands and a level line with no tick numbers. All names, descriptions, and the title are added later by an interactive HTML overlay driven by a separate data file. If any lettering or numbers appear anywhere in the picture, regenerate.

## Image Specifications

- **Format:** PNG
- **Dimensions:** 1920 × 1080 px, landscape, 16:9 ratio. If the model cannot produce this exact size, generate at any 16:9 size, because the overlay uses percentages and will still line up.
- **Background:** Warm off-white (#FAF7F2) with a very faint blueprint grid.
- **Audience:** Graduate students, postdocs, and faculty in electrical and computer engineering.
- **Style:** High-quality, polished, semi-realistic illustration with soft lighting from the upper left, rich detail, and a consistent line weight. Saturated but professional colors. Agents are small friendly teal (#1F7A8C) workers with oval heads and no faces, never humanoid robots with faces. The one human is faceless or softly stylized. No glowing brains, no chrome robot hands, no stock-photo clichés.
- **Palette:** maroon #7A0019 for the researcher and the human gate; gold #FFCC33 for review checkpoints and focal points; teal #1F7A8C for the agents; signal green #3C8D5A for accepted or shared work; brick red #C0392B for conflict and overload; amber #B9770E for caution; charcoal #2A2E3A for structure. Always pair color with an icon or pattern, never color alone.

## What to Draw

One wide organization view, like a theater stage seen from the audience, drawn on a single cream canvas with **no panel borders**. The stage has **three horizontal tiers** and one tall gauge on the far right. Keep a safe margin of about 2% on every side. Do not add a title bar, header, footer, legend, or side panel.

- **Top tier (about 3% to 30% from top):** a wide bank of about twenty small teal planning agents at desks, arranged in two rows of ten between roughly 12% and 74% from left. Each holds or sits beside a rolled map, an outline sheet, a checklist, or a specification page.
- **Middle tier (about 33% to 70% from top):** a flowing conveyor belt of task cards and a shared table below it.
- **Bottom tier (about 71% to 87% from top):** a row of ten sturdier, darker teal execution agents at benches between roughly 8% and 80% from left, working on code, analysis, simulation, figures, and manuscript pages. They are visibly larger and fewer than the planners above.
- **Front of stage:** at the center of the lower edge, a maroon (#7A0019) figure with a baton stands behind a podium.
- **Far right:** a tall gauge for the researcher's attention.

Planners are drawn lighter teal with small hats or visors; executors are darker teal with tool belts. All agents have simple oval heads and no faces.

## Layout Notes

- Markers sit on the visual center of the thing they name, as given in the marker map. Leave a little empty cream around each target so a marker dot and label do not hide key detail.
- No text anywhere. Use pictograms, shapes, arrows, and colored bars only.
- The belt runs left to right at about 36% to 50% from top. The Review Checkpoint arch is low and the Human Approval Gate is tall, so they read as two different stops.
- The three tiers are evenly spaced, with the planners above, the belt in the middle, and the doers below, so the viewer can count about twenty planners and ten doers.
- Keep line weight, rounding, and shading (light from the upper left) consistent across the picture.
- The key idea is that the most scarce thing on the stage is the researcher's attention. Make the single maroon figure and the draining gauge the strongest contrast against the many teal agents.

## Do Not Include

- Any text, letters, numbers, labels, captions, or meter numerals
- A title, heading, legend, banner, or callout lines
- Readable code, file names, or screen content
- Photorealistic faces, or humanoid robots with faces
- Brand names, logos, a watermark, or a signature
