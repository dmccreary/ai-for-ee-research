## About This MicroSim

A general principle such as "disclose substantive AI use" is hard to act on until it is written as specific rules and tried on real cases. This MicroSim gives you a five-rule lab policy and asks you to **apply** it to eight scenarios from an electrical engineering project. The policy is an *illustrative lab policy, not a real venue's policy*. Real journal, conference, and institutional policies differ and change, so read the current author guidelines of each venue at submission time.

| Rule | Statement |
|---|---|
| 1 | Text drafted or substantially rewritten by an AI tool that appears in a submitted manuscript is disclosed in the paper. |
| 2 | AI use to generate or analyze data, code, or figures that contribute to reported results is disclosed in the paper, with the tool and version. |
| 3 | Grammar, spelling, and formatting assistance does not need to be disclosed. |
| 4 | AI use for brainstorming or literature search that does not appear in the paper is recorded in the project log only. |
| 5 | An AI system is never listed as an author. |

For each scenario you choose one of four actions: **Disclose in the paper**, **Record in the project log only**, **No disclosure needed**, or **Not permitted**. The policy has gradations on purpose, so that you separate a disclosure in the paper from a project log entry and from no record at all. After the eighth scenario the sim assembles a draft disclosure statement from the scenarios that required disclosure in the paper. The draft is illustrative and is not legal advice: the tool name, model version, and date of use are left as placeholders to fill in from your project log.

**Learning objective:** apply a five-rule illustrative lab disclosure policy to eight scenarios of AI use and give the required action for each, with at least 7 of 8 correct.

## How to Use

1. Read the policy at the top and scenario 1 in the dark-blue box. The question on screen is *What does the policy require here?*
2. Choose an action from the drop-down list below the drawing area and press **Submit**. Each scenario has one attempt, and the answer cannot be changed after you submit it.
3. Read the feedback. A correct answer shows **Correct.** with a check mark; an incorrect answer shows **The policy gives** the required action, with an X. Both show the rule number and the reason, and the matching rule is outlined and marked with an arrow in the policy.
4. Press **Next Scenario** to continue. The scenarios come in a fixed order.
5. After scenario 8, press **See Score**. The summary shows *n of 8 correct*, whether you met the mastery threshold of 7, and a draft disclosure statement built from the scenarios that required disclosure in the paper. Reload the page to start again.

## Lesson Plan

### Learning Objective
The learner will apply a five-rule illustrative lab disclosure policy to eight scenarios of AI use and give the required action for each, with at least 7 of 8 correct.

### Bloom Level
Apply (Bloom verb: **apply**)

### Grade Level
Graduate students (MS and PhD) in electrical engineering, computer engineering, and computer design, plus postdocs and faculty who want practical AI skills for everyday research

### Duration
10 minutes

### Prerequisites
- AI disclosure, citation of AI use, and authorship policy, from the section "Integrity, Disclosure, and Authorship" of Chapter 18
- The audit trail from Chapter 17

### Common Misconceptions Addressed
- Only AI-written text needs disclosure.
- If AI was used heavily, it can be listed as an author.
- Any use at all must be disclosed in the paper.

### Evidence of Mastery
- For each of the eight scenarios the learner commits one of the four actions. An action is correct when it equals the action the stated policy gives.
- Mastery is at least 7 of 8 correct on the first attempt.
- Reading the policy is exploration and is not evidence of mastery.
