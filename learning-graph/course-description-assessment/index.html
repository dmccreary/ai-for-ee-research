    Gap Analysis¶

    1. Topic count and overlap (-1). Topics 7 (planning and execution agents) and 8 (orchestration) cover closely related ground. They can stay separate for concept generation, but consider grouping the 16 topics under four or five themes for chapter planning.
    2. EE specificity in Apply (-1). The outcomes read as generally applicable to any research field. Adding two or three EE and computer-design tasks (for example generating a testbench, scripting a parameter sweep, or cleaning measurement data) would anchor the learning graph in the audience's domain.
    3. Named case studies in Analyze (-1). The case-study outcome refers to case studies generically. Naming them (for example the MicroSims arXiv paper and a specific intelligent textbook) would let the graph include concrete case-study concepts.

    Improvement Suggestions¶

    Prioritized by their effect on concept generation:

    1. Add two or three EE or computer-design examples to the Apply level.
    2. Name the specific case studies once the talk content is final.
    3. Optionally add a short "Course Format" section (duration, hands-on versus lecture, expected tools). The rubric does not score it, but it helps chapter design.

    Concept Generation Readiness¶

    Estimated concepts: about 220-260.

    • 16 topics at an average of 12-16 concepts each gives roughly 190-260 concepts.
    • Six Bloom's levels with 4-6 outcomes each suggest a healthy mix of vocabulary, procedural, and evaluative concepts.
    • Topics 4 and 5 (Markdown and skills) and topic 14 (verification) are the densest and are likely to produce the most concepts.
    • Likely to fall short only if the case-study chapter stays generic.

    Next Steps¶

    Score is at or above 85, so the course description is ready for learning graph generation. Recommended next skill: learning-graph-generator.
