    Appendix: Agent Email¶

    Research notes — not yet a finished chapter

    This appendix holds research gathered on October 21, 2026, as a follow-up to Personal Agent Software. Most claims are vendor-stated or come from press and vendor blogs. Only the Grok Bot inbox was tested first-hand (by the author, in October 2026), and every other entry is untested. Features in this area changed within days of announcement, so verify against each vendor's own pages before relying on anything here.

    Why an agent's own address matters¶

    An always-on agent that can only use your mailbox acts as you. That has two costs for a research group:

    • No separation. Mail the agent sends comes from your address, and mail it reads is your private mail, including anything unpublished.
    • Stalled sign-ups. Several products filter one-time codes, password-reset links, and magic links out of the connected mailbox, so an agent cannot finish creating an account on a service for you.

    An address of the agent's own fixes both problems. People can also email the agent like a colleague, forward it a message, or add it to a thread. The cost is a new way in for attackers: any message sent to that address is untrusted input the agent will read.

    Prompt injection through the inbox

    A malicious email can carry hidden instructions aimed at the agent. Treat anything that arrives at an agent-owned address as untrusted data, keep approval rules on for sending and spending, and do not give the agent standing access to unpublished research data it does not need.

    Summary¶

    Three terms recur below. A built-in address is one the vendor provisions. A bolt-on address comes from a separate service you connect yourself. A text channel is any way to message the agent from a phone, such as SMS, iMessage, or WhatsApp.

    Grok Bot (SpaceXAI)¶

    Grok Bot is the one product here that has shipped a built-in inbox for its bots, and it is the only one the author has tested.

    Email¶

    • Announcement. The official @bot account on X announced on October 9, 2026 that a bot can now have its own inbox. Elon Musk replied "Yes" when asked if it was real; that one-word reply is the only executive confirmation we found. SpaceXAI posted it on X and we found no help page or release notes.
    • Address. Every address ends in @mail.grokbot.com, and the user chooses the name in front of the @. You claim it by asking the bot in chat or by tagging @bot on X. The bot checks that the name is free and asks for your approval first.
    • Permanent names. One write-up says addresses are unique and are never reissued, even after you delete them, so choose carefully.
    • What it is for. The stated uses are signing up for services (receiving the confirmation link or one-time code and finishing the sign-up), contacting businesses to book or request quotes, and arranging meeting times by email.
    • Team plans. An admin must switch the feature on before members can use it.
    • Author test. The author sent a test message to a bot's address and the bot replied from that same address.

    Text messaging¶

    We found no reporting of SMS, iMessage, or a phone number for Grok Bot. Work is assigned in a chat window on desktop or iOS. Because each bot has a cloud computer that signs in to websites, it could in principle register for a third-party phone-number service, but we did not test that and found no documentation of it.

    Bolt-on options¶

    • An AgentMail plugin has been available to Grok Bot users since August 2026, before the built-in address. Install it from Settings → Plugins, or add AgentMail as a custom MCP connector with an API key. The AgentMail page covers email only and says Grok Bot access for it is limited to the higher plans.
    • Out of the box, Grok Bot can read a mailbox you connect through Gmail or Outlook, but that mailbox is yours, not the bot's.

    Open questions¶

    • Pricing, sending limits, and country restrictions for the inbox are unpublished.
    • It is not documented whether each outgoing email or sign-up needs approval.
    • SpaceXAI's terms make the user responsible for "agentic actions," and those include sending communications. A bot that emails the wrong business is the user's problem.
    • Practical limits of about 50 bots per account and six per group chat were reported with the announcement.

    Dots (OpenAI)¶

    Email¶

    No, not at launch. OpenAI's help article "Getting started with your dot" is quoted by a search tool as saying a dot cannot have its own standalone email address at launch. A dot can instead use your personal email account, which you connect for your tasks. Custom rules can restrict that use, for example telling a dot never to send email.

    A follow-up report dated October 1, 2026 said OpenAI had not changed dots' pricing, regions, or email addresses since the September 29 launch. We found nothing newer.

    Source limit

    OpenAI's help pages returned HTTP 403 to our fetches, so we did not read them ourselves. The quoted wording above reaches us through a search summary. Open the OpenAI help article yourself before relying on it.

    Text messaging¶

    Sources conflict, so treat all of this as unconfirmed:

    • OpenAI said dots will "soon" be available on other messaging apps and over the phone, without naming apps or dates.
    • Agent Sonic, a competing product vendor, lists SMS as a limited beta for U.S. Pro users, with carrier charges possible, and says WhatsApp is not a launch channel. It cites no OpenAI page.
    • Tom's Guide and MarkTechPost describe texting as planned.
    • OpenAI's Enterprise help page is reported to say phone messaging through iMessage, RCS, or WhatsApp is unavailable for Enterprise at launch.
    • A dot cannot place phone calls to the user at launch, and setup is desktop-only.
    • Slack and Microsoft Teams integrations were announced.

    Bolt-on options¶

    AgentMail publishes a guide for giving a dot its own address: connect AgentMail in the conversation and the dot creates an inbox it can send from and receive at. That is vendor marketing for a product the vendor sells, and we did not test it.

    Muse (Meta)¶

    Email¶

    Announced, not shipped. At Meta Connect on September 23, 2026, Alexandr Wang said each Muse agent will get its own email address "soon," giving no date. TechCrunch reports it is still in development and quotes the reasoning that an agent running in the background will naturally need one. Users will be able to forward messages to the agent or add it to a thread.

    • Domain unknown. TechCrunch does not say what the address will look like. One outlet describes it as an @muse.ai inbox, which we could not confirm.
    • Why it matters here. Muse's Gmail connector filters out one-time tokens, password-reset links, and login magic links, so account sign-ups can stall. That is the gap a separate inbox would fill.
    • Today. Meta's own launch post describes only connecting your existing email, and lets you choose whether Muse reads your mail or can also send on your behalf.

    Text messaging¶

    Meta's launch post says talking to Muse "works just like messaging another person," in the Muse app or directly in WhatsApp. You link WhatsApp under Settings → Messaging channels. Muse is separate from the free Meta AI assistant already inside WhatsApp. We found no SMS channel and no dedicated phone number for Muse; results about WhatsApp numbers concerned Meta's business agents, a different product.

    Bolt-on options¶

    AgentMail publishes a setup guide that works today: create an API key, paste it into the Secrets tab Muse opens (not into the chat), and approve the connection. Muse then creates its own inbox, by default on agentmail.to, or on a custom domain with a paid AgentMail plan. This is vendor material and we did not test it. The Secrets-tab design is a useful detail: the key never passes through the model's chat context.

    Claude Cowork (Anthropic)¶

    Email¶

    No. Anthropic's Cowork help article mentions no agent address, no inbox others can email, and no agent email feature. Cowork reaches mail only through the same connectors as Claude chat, such as Gmail and Microsoft 365, and those act on your account.

    • Sending is supervised. Anthropic's help center says the Gmail connector can send, reply to, and forward, and asks for approval before each action by default. Team and Enterprise owners decide whether members can let those actions run without asking.
    • Conflicting claims. One Claude docs page describes the Gmail connector as read-only, and a third-party blog says Cowork sends only through Microsoft
    • These conflict with the help center, which was updated most recently.
    • No inbound work. One comparison says nobody outside your Claude account can send Cowork work, and every task, including scheduled ones, starts with you.
    • A hint, not a feature. A RuntimeWire report from October 19, 2026 says Claude's web code contains a new organization-level setting for CC'ing Claude on email. The report found no address, workflow, or rollout date, and Anthropic has confirmed nothing.

    Text messaging¶

    Cowork's phone channel is Dispatch, which gives you one persistent thread between the Claude mobile app and Claude Desktop. Your computer does the work and must stay awake and online. Dispatch launched as a research preview on March 17, 2026, and Anthropic's current article lists it as unavailable to new users. We found no SMS, iMessage, or Telegram support.

    Bolt-on options¶

    AgentMail, Email Bump, and Carly each sell dedicated agent inboxes that connect to Cowork through connectors or MCP. We saw them only in search results, which call out Email Bump addresses on misomail.co. All three are commercial claims that we did not verify. Cowork supports MCP connectors, which is the extension point that makes these workable.

    Gemini Spark (Google)¶

    Email¶

    Yes, press-reported. The Next Web says users "can email Spark directly through a dedicated Gmail address, much as they would message a human colleague." Other sources describe Spark as a teammate with its own Gmail address. None gave the address itself or the setup steps, and we read no Google page on it. It is the only mainstream product here where the address is part of the original launch, announced May 19, 2026 at Google I/O.

    Google's Workspace tie-in helps here: Spark already reaches Gmail, Docs, and Calendar without manual setup. Press says it asks before high-stakes actions such as sending email, and that Google connectors are off until you enable them.

    Text messaging¶

    Announced, not shipped. Google's I/O materials list texting Spark, along with emailing it, creating custom sub-agents, and operating your local browser, among abilities coming later. We found no report that texting was live. The Next Web article does not mention texting at all.

    Bolt-on options¶

    None are needed for email. We did not look for a bolt-on text service for Spark.

    Adding email or texting yourself¶

    A growing set of services give any agent a mailbox or a phone number, provided the agent can call tools. Which products allow this differs:

    Need Service Notes
    Email inbox AgentMail Gives an agent a real inbox with threading and webhooks. Guides exist for Grok Bot, Dots, Muse, and Claude. Vendor-published.
    Email inbox Email Bump, Carly Named in search results for Cowork. Not examined.
    SMS and voice AgentPhone Y Combinator-backed. Provisions a U.S. or Canadian number per agent through one API call. Vendor claims.
    SMS and voice Dial Claims a number in about ten seconds and says it can read inbound codes for phone-gated sign-ups. Names Grok Bot and Claude Code as clients. Vendor claims.
    SMS and voice Twilio The do-it-yourself route. Hermes Agent ships a telephony skill that can buy a Twilio number and send and receive SMS.

    None of the text services above were tested, and our search found no Muse- or Dots-specific SMS guide. Two cautions apply to all of them:

    • Cost and abuse. An agent with a purchasable number can spend money and message strangers. Keep spending limits and require approval to send.
    • Verification codes. A number that receives sign-up codes lets the agent create accounts on its own. Decide whether that is something you want an unattended agent to do.

    Caveats and gaps¶

    • No first-party reading for Dots or Spark. OpenAI's help pages returned HTTP 403, and no Google page on Spark's address was read.
    • Vendor-heavy sources. Most "how to add email" guides are published by the companies that sell the inbox, and they rank their own product.
    • One tested claim. Only the Grok Bot inbox was verified first-hand. The author has not tried any bolt-on service.
    • Fast-moving. The Grok Bot announcement was twelve days old and the Claude CC report two days old when this was written.
    • Text channels differ in kind. WhatsApp, Dispatch, Slack, and SMS differ in who can message the agent and who pays. We did not compare them in detail.

    Questions to resolve before this becomes a chapter¶

    1. Does each agent require approval before sending an email or signing up for a service, and can that be set per recipient?
    2. How does each vendor treat mail the agent receives: logged, retained, used for training, or visible to admins?
    3. Which agent could safely hold a lab-group address that collaborators email with data requests?
    4. What should a research group's rule be for an agent that can sign up for outside services on its own?
